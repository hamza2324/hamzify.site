Some links on Hamzify are affiliate links. This page explains what that means, where those
links appear, and what it does not change.

## The disclosure

If you click an affiliate link and buy or subscribe to something, Hamzify may receive a
commission. It costs you nothing extra — the price is the same as going to the site
directly.

Affiliate links are marked in two ways: articles containing them show a notice at the top,
and every affiliate link carries `rel="sponsored nofollow"` in the markup so search engines
and readers using extensions can identify them.

## What it does not change

This is the part that matters.

- **Coverage is not for sale.** No company can pay to be reviewed, to be reviewed sooner,
  or to be included in a comparison.
- **Verdicts are not for sale.** A tool with an affiliate programme gets the same treatment
  as one without. Several tools recommended on this site have no affiliate programme at
  all, and some tools with generous programmes are recommended against.
- **Negative conclusions still get published.** If a tool with an active affiliate
  relationship is the wrong choice for a use case, the article says so.
- **No affiliate link is added retroactively to change a conclusion.**

If a piece of coverage was paid for or sponsored in any way, it will say so at the top, in
plain language, and it will be labelled as sponsored — not blended in with editorial. As of
the last update to this page, no sponsored content has been published on Hamzify.

## Why affiliate links at all

Testing tools properly costs money. Subscriptions, API credits and paid tiers are real
expenses, and hands-on testing is the entire value of this site. Affiliate income offsets
that without putting articles behind a paywall or filling pages with display ads.

The alternative funding models were considered and rejected for now: display advertising
degrades reading, and sponsored posts compromise the thing that makes tool coverage worth
reading.

## Programmes

Where Hamzify participates in an affiliate programme for a tool discussed on the site, the
relationship is disclosed on the article in question. If a programme requires specific
wording — for example the Amazon Associates disclosure — that wording appears on the
relevant pages.
