Technical writing about fast-moving tools gets things wrong. What matters is what happens
next. This page explains how corrections work on Hamzify.

## How to report something

Email [hamzajadoon71@gmail.com](mailto:hamzajadoon71@gmail.com) with the page URL and what looks
wrong. Every article footer has a "Send a correction" link that pre-fills this for you.

You do not need to be polite about it, and you do not need to be certain. A "this looks off"
message that turns out to be right is worth more than a wrong claim staying up.

## What counts as a correction

**Factual errors** — a wrong version number, a misattributed feature, an incorrect price, a
broken code sample, a mischaracterisation of what a tool does. These get fixed.

**Things that have changed** — a tool ships a feature that a review said was missing, or
pricing changes. These are updates rather than corrections: the article gets revised and its
"Updated" date changes.

**Disagreement about a conclusion** — genuinely welcome, but a difference of opinion is not
an error. If your experience contradicts what is published, that is useful context and may
be added to the article with credit.

## How corrections are handled

| Severity | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Typo, broken link, formatting | Fixed silently |
| Factual error that does not change the conclusion | Fixed, and a correction note added at the end of the article with the date |
| Factual error that changes the conclusion | Fixed, correction note at the **top** of the article, and the "Updated" date changed |
| Article is fundamentally wrong | Article is retracted with an explanation left in place at the same URL |

The target is to respond within a few days and to fix confirmed errors as soon as they are
verified.

## What will not happen

- **Quiet rewriting.** A conclusion will not be changed without saying that it changed.
- **Deleting the URL.** Retracted articles keep their address with an explanation, because
  breaking a link that people have shared hides the mistake instead of owning it.
- **Removing a correction later** to make the archive look cleaner.

## Public history

This site is built from files in a public repository, so the full edit history of every
article is available and independently checkable. That is deliberate: it makes the
corrections process verifiable rather than a promise.

## Updates versus corrections

An "Updated" date means the article was revisited and revised — usually because the tool
changed. It does not imply an error was found. Where a substantive claim changed, the note
in the article says what and why.
